# What treatments are prescribed based on Hims client needs assessment

Hims uses the intake to route people into a set of standardized prescription and non‑prescription treatment bundles, mainly around hair loss, ED, skin and mental health, with exact choice depending on symptoms, severity, history and preferences.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]

## Hair loss treatments

From the hair‑loss “quiz” and medical questionnaire, typical outcomes are:

- **Oral finasteride**: daily tablet for male pattern hair loss, blocks DHT to slow or reverse androgenetic alopecia.[^6][^2][^5]
- **Topical minoxidil**: drops, foam or solution applied to scalp, often for mild–moderate or early hair loss.[^5][^6][^1]
- **Combo Rx products**:
    - Topical finasteride + minoxidil sprays or serums (e.g., 0.3% finasteride + 6% minoxidil) for men who want a “one‑step” regimen.[^7][^1]
    - “Hybrid” compounded tablets combining finasteride with supplements (biotin, vitamins, etc.).[^8]
- **Non‑Rx support** (often bundled with Rx): thickening shampoos, conditioners, biotin gummies, DHT‑blocking shampoos.[^2][^5]

Which option they land on depends on: pattern and duration of hair loss, previous treatments, tolerance for systemic vs topical, and medical red flags; the intake lets the clinician choose or decline these.[^4][^6][^1]

## Erectile dysfunction (ED) treatments

For ED questionnaires (sexual health, cardiovascular risk, meds, etc.), prescribers commonly use:

- **Sildenafil** (generic Viagra): taken “as needed,” onset around 30 minutes, duration about 4 hours.[^9][^10][^4]
- **Tadalafil** (generic Cialis): either daily low‑dose or as‑needed, effects up to about 36 hours.[^10][^9][^4]

The intake helps rule out contraindications (e.g., nitrates, severe cardiac risk) and choose dose and regimen (daily vs on‑demand, sildenafil vs tadalafil).[^10][^4]

## Mental health and sleep treatments

For anxiety, depression, and related concerns, Hims may prescribe non‑controlled psychotropic medications after an online assessment:

- **SSRIs**: sertraline, fluoxetine, citalopram, escitalopram, etc.[^11][^3]
- **Other antidepressants / anxiolytics**: bupropion XL, duloxetine, venlafaxine, buspirone, and similar.[^3][^11]
- **OTC supports**: “calm” oral drops, sleep gummies, etc., sometimes recommended without Rx or as adjuncts.[^3]

They explicitly do not prescribe controlled substances like benzodiazepines or stimulants.[^11]

## Skin, infections, allergies and other areas

Using similar symptom‑based questionnaires, Hims can also prescribe or recommend:

- **Acne / skin**: topical retinoids, antibiotic or combination creams, other prescription acne treatments, plus OTC skincare routines.[^4]
- **Allergies**: antihistamines and allergy symptom treatments, sometimes Rx, sometimes OTC.[^4]
- **Infections**: treatments for UTIs, fungal infections (ringworm, jock itch, athlete’s foot, yeast infections), and others, depending on local regulation.[^4]

Again, the intake determines whether they can safely treat via telehealth or must redirect to in‑person care.[^4]

## How this maps conceptually

Conceptually, each Hims “quiz + consultation” narrows the user into:

- A **condition category** (hair loss, ED, anxiety, acne…).[^4]
- A **severity/complexity tier**, excluding red flags.[^6][^11][^4]
- A **treatment lane**: full prescription path, OTC only, or “no Rx possible → see in‑person doctor.”[^11][^4]

Within a lane, they mostly choose between a small catalog of standard molecules and bundle them into subscription‑friendly regimens (often combining Rx + OTC).[^1][^2][^3]
